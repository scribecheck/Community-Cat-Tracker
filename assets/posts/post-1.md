Let's talk about how to take care of local community cats in winter. When we see our furry friends outside in the cold, it's natural to worry about them and want to do something to help. You'll be surprised how easy it is!

## What do community cats need in winter?

**Fresh, clean water**
Many usual water sources dry up or freeze over in the winter. Provide your community cats with clean, fresh water every day so they don't drink dirty water out of desperation.
Tip: Use a sturdy bowl that won't easily blow away or get knocked over.

**Wet and dry food**
It's tiring to live outside in the cold. Your community cats will appreciate regular meals that provide them with necessary calories. Wet food will also help to keep them hydrated.
Tip: Try to put the food out at the same time(s) every day so the cat gets used to the schedule. Be careful not to leave wet food out for a long time!

**Waterproof cat shelter**
You can buy a cat shelter or make one! I use styrofoam coolers from the home center because they're waterproof and cheap, and it's easy to cut a hole in it for the cat. There are lots of great tutorials online and on YouTube that you can follow to make your own cat shelter.
Tip: Did you know that straw is better than blankets for keeping cats warm in winter? This is because blankets can absorb moisture and might freeze. However, straw is better at repelling water (even if the cat is damp from rain or snow).

**TNR (Trap, Neuter/Spay, Release)**
Have you seen community cats with a little triangle cut on the tips of their ears? It looks like a sakura petal, right? This is how you know the cat has already been trapped and taken to the vet to be vaccinated and sterilized. This practice is called "TNR" and it means "Trapt the cat," "Neuter or spay the cat," and then "Release the cat back into the exact same area where you trapped it." There are many diseases and health risks associated with mating and giving birth, which is why TNR is one of the best ways to help community cats live safer, better lives outdoors. Especially in winter!
Tip: Matsudo City offers subsidies for TNR vet costs! You can find information about the program and a list of affiliated veterinary offices on the city website here. You can also research vets who specialize in helping community cats, such as Inagaki Animal Hospital in Koshigaya, Saitama.
